Workshop Abstract

Bridging Physical Interaction with Tactile World Models

Touch provides robots with direct access to physical interaction, revealing contact, force, friction, slip, compliance, deformation, and object dynamics that cannot always be inferred from vision alone. As robotics moves toward Embodied AI and Physical AI, an emerging challenge is to develop Tactile World Models that enable robots not only to perceive contact, but to learn the dynamics of physical interaction, predict the consequences of their actions, and use these predictions for adaptive grasping and manipulation.

Scientific Motivation

Why Vision Is Not Enough: The Tactile Simulation Gap

Visual perception suffers from severe line-of-sight occlusions during contact-rich tasks like bimanual assembly, wiping, or gripping delicate objects. Vision cannot measure shear force, friction distribution, or minute surface deformation. Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM) close this gap by learning true interaction dynamics in latent space.

Long-Horizon Rollout Prediction Error

Comparing Latent State Error over Manipulation Timesteps

* Quantitative evidence demonstrating how integrating tactile feedback into world models prevents exponential rollout divergence during unobserved contact events.

❌

Vision-Only Limitations

  • Line-of-Sight Occlusion: Hand/gripper completely blocks object contact interfaces.
  • Force Blindness: Unable to observe shear, pressure, or frictional limits prior to slip.
  • Tactile Simulation Gap: Synthetic renders fail to replicate non-linear material compliance.
✔

Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM)

  • Unmediated Physical Access: Direct measurement of micro-geometries and contact dynamics.
  • Predictive Slippage Modeling: Latent states anticipate slip states 100ms before movement.
  • Active Inference & Control: Real-time torque and positional adaptation during grasp execution.
International Sim-to-Real Challenge

The RoboTac 2027 Sim-to-Real Benchmark

To systematically evaluate emerging Tactile World Models, RoboTac 2027 together with Daimon Robotics is distributing 20 hardware sets to selected global academic and research laboratories. Teams will pre-train on the RobOmni Isaac Sim environment and validate on real physical hardware.

01

DM-DataClaw

A specialized Data-as-a-Service (DaaS) instrument engineered for unconstrained, handheld multimodal tactile demonstration and high-fidelity data collection across diverse environments without platform locking.

Modality: Stereo Vision + Wrist Fisheye + High-Density Tactile Array + 6-Axis IMU
02

DM-TacClaw

An electrically actuated tactile robotic gripper maintaining strict isomorphic structural parity to the DM-DataClaw, enabling zero-shot deployment of learned world models onto real physical arms.

Specs: 100mm stroke, 0.1mm position precision, uint16 raw tactile video output
03

RobOmni Isaac Sim

Jointly engineered by Daimon Robotics and Galbot on NVIDIA Isaac Sim. Offers an exact 1:1 digital twin of the DM-TacClaw gripper with contact physics, shear modeling, and material compliance.

Platform: Public access for global community & benchmarking participants

Benchmark Evaluation Criteria & Scoring Matrix

Submissions are evaluated across three standardized dimensions

1. Simulated Control Efficacy (30%)

Success rate, execution velocity, and contact stability within RobOmni Isaac Sim across standardized grasping, wiping, and assembly tasks.

2. Sim-to-Real Generalization (40%)

Zero-shot and few-shot transfer performance from digital twin to real DM-TacClaw hardware under unmodeled real-world friction and deformability.

3. Predictive Fidelity Metrics (30%)

Quantitative evaluation of latent state prediction accuracy for future tactile frames, shear vectors, and slip detection over 100+ timesteps.

PyTorch Tensor Preprocessing Advisory uint16 → float32 
import torch
import torchvision.io as io

# Read uint16 FFV1 video feed from DM-TacClaw
video_tensor = io.read_video("gripper_left_tactile.mov")[0]

# CRITICAL: Convert uint16 integers to float32 [0.0, 1.0]
# Prevent gradient explosion during World Model latent training
tactile_float = video_tensor.to(torch.float32) / 65535.0

print(f"Decoded Latent Tensor Shape: {tactile_float.shape}")
# Output: torch.Size([Frames, 480, 640, 3])

* Note for researchers: Raw integer values must be normalized prior to feeding into world model autoregressive transformers.
