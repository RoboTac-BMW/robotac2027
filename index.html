Overview & Scientific Motivation

Bridging Physical Interaction with Tactile World Models

Touch provides robots with direct access to physical interaction, revealing contact, force, friction, slip, compliance, deformation, and object dynamics that cannot always be inferred from vision alone. As robotics moves toward Embodied AI and Physical AI, an emerging challenge is to develop Tactile World Models that enable robots not only to perceive contact, but to learn the dynamics of physical interaction, predict the consequences of their actions, and use these predictions for adaptive grasping and manipulation.

Visual perception suffers from severe line-of-sight occlusions during contact-rich tasks like bimanual assembly, wiping, or gripping delicate objects. Vision cannot measure shear force, friction distribution, or minute surface deformation. Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM) close this gap by learning true interaction dynamics in latent space.

International Sim-to-Real Challenge

Sim-to-Real Benchmark & Hardware Ecosystem

To systematically evaluate emerging Tactile World Models, RoboTac 2027 together with Daimon Robotics is distributing 20 hardware sets to selected global academic and research laboratories. Designed with strict structural isomorphism between human data acquisition and robotic actuation, teams will pre-train on the digital twin and validate on real physical hardware.

DM-DataClaw

A specialized Data-as-a-Service (DaaS) instrument engineered for unconstrained, handheld multimodal tactile demonstration.

Stereo Vision + Wrist Fisheye + Tactile + IMU

DM-TacClaw

An electrically actuated tactile robotic gripper maintaining strict isomorphic structural parity to the DM-DataClaw.

100mm stroke, 0.1mm precision, uint16
[ Isaac Sim Twin ]

RobOmni

Offers an exact 1:1 digital twin of the DM-TacClaw gripper with contact physics and material compliance.

Public access for community benchmarking

Benchmark Evaluation Criteria

Submissions are evaluated across three standardized dimensions without requiring local hardware.

1. Simulated Efficacy (30%)

Success rate, execution velocity, and contact stability within RobOmni Isaac Sim across grasping and wiping tasks.

2. Sim-to-Real Transfer (40%)

Zero-shot and few-shot transfer performance from digital twin to real DM-TacClaw hardware under real-world friction.

3. Predictive Fidelity (30%)

Quantitative evaluation of latent state prediction accuracy for future tactile frames, shear vectors, and slip detection.

✓ Topics of Interest

  • Architectural design & scaling of Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM).
  • Predictive coding and active inference for contact-rich manipulation.
  • Sim-to-real policy transfer & closing the tactile simulation gap.
